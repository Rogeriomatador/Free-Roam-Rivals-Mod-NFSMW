# Vision — Rockport should feel inhabited

## Product statement

Free Roam Rivals turns NFSMW free roam into a persistent street-racing ecosystem.

The player should stop thinking of rivals as spawned race objects and start thinking of them as other racers who happen to live in the same city.

## Design principles

### 1. Rivals exist before the player interacts with them

A rival should be able to drive, cross districts, pass the player, disappear down another road, meet another racer, or refuse a challenge.

The player is not the center of every rival's existence.

### 2. Challenges should feel physical, not menu-driven

The ideal flow:

```text
spot rival
-> rival notices player
-> rival changes behavior
-> player approaches
-> horn / gesture
-> rival acknowledges
-> staging
-> wager
-> race
```

A giant permanent marker is a fallback, not the core interaction.

### 3. No loading screen for ordinary street races

The race is an overlay on the existing free-roam world.

Traffic, weather, police and world streaming remain active.

### 4. Failure creates stories

Losing money is meaningful.

Losing a car is memorable.

Seeing the same rival later driving the car they won from you is the kind of systemic consequence this project is trying to create.

### 5. NPCs follow the same important rules

If the player may not wager their last car, neither may a rival.

If rivals have cash, wagers reduce it.

If a rival loses cars, their virtual garage changes.

### 6. Personality should matter more than raw difficulty

Difficulty is not "give the AI 300% speed."

A rival can differ by:

- aggression
- confidence
- clean/dirty racing tendency
- police fear
- willingness to wager cars
- grudge
- respect
- risk tolerance
- preferred districts
- preferred race type
- mistake rate

### 7. The mod must coexist with heavily modded installations

The core ASI should avoid replacing stock game archives wherever possible.

Optional integrations must be isolated and feature-gated.

## Long-term fantasy

A player should eventually be able to recognize a car before reading a name:

> "That red Mustang is the guy who beat me yesterday."

The city becomes a network of relationships rather than a static map.
